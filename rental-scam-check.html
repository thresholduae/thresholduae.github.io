Is this rental ad a scam? 9 checks before you pay anything

Fake rental ads in the UAE follow the same script: a great flat, a low price, and a reason you have to pay before you see it. These are the signs to look for, and what to do when you see one.

The 9 warning signs

  1. You're asked to pay before a viewing. A "holding deposit" or "booking fee" to reserve a flat you haven't seen is the most common trick. Pay nothing until you've been inside and checked who owns it.
  2. The landlord is abroad. "Owner is overseas, the keys will be couriered" means you can't meet anyone or see the unit. That is exactly why scammers use it.
  3. The price is far below the area. A two-bedroom at a studio price in Marina or Downtown isn't a lucky find. Compare it with three similar listings in the same building.
  4. Hurry. "Many people interested, first to pay gets it" exists to stop you from checking anything.
  5. No permit number. Property ads in Dubai are meant to carry an advertising permit number from the Dubai Land Department. If it's missing, ask for it.
  6. Payment to a personal account, in cash or by wire. Rent and deposits normally go by cheque or transfer to the landlord or the agency named in the contract.
  7. No broker card. Licensed agents in Dubai carry a broker card and can tell you their registration number. Ask to see it at the viewing.
  8. Photos that don't match. Reverse-search the pictures. Scam ads often reuse photos from hotel pages or other listings.
  9. They avoid Ejari. A real tenancy gets registered with Ejari. If someone says "no need to register", walk away.

Before you hand over money

  • View the home in person and check that the unit number matches the ad.
  • Ask for the title deed and the owner's ID copy, or the agency's authority to rent it out.
  • Make sure the names on the contract, the title deed and the cheques all match.
  • Agree in writing how and when the deposit comes back.

If you've already paid

Keep every message, receipt and the ad itself, and report it straight away: in Dubai, to Dubai Police (the eCrime platform or 901), and to the Dubai Land Department if a broker or agency was involved. The sooner it's reported, the better the chance of tracing the money.
